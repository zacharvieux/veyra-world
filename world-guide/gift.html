The Mark & Gift

Every mortal eventually reaches a Coming of Age when the Gift awakens.

The timing varies from person to person and usually falls somewhere in the broad transition from childhood into adolescence. No culture can reliably force it to happen early.

The Mark

When the Gift first awakens, a visible Mark of the Gift appears.

Its first manifestation is unmistakable. The Mark may glow, shimmer, pulse, or appear illuminated beneath skin, fur, feathers, or scales.

It is normally not painful, though people report sensations such as warmth, cold, tingling, pressure, or heightened awareness.

Over the following days or weeks, the brilliance fades, leaving a pale scar or faint pattern that remains for life.

Across all peoples:

  • a Fixed Mark appears complete, contained, and clearly finished;
  • an Open Mark appears branching, broken, reaching, or visibly unfinished.

The Mark tells a person that the Gift has awakened and whether it is Fixed or Open. It normally does not reveal the exact ability.

Elves — The Living Vine

An elven Mark appears as a vine-like pattern somewhere on the body.

A Fixed Mark forms a completed vine, leaf, bud, curl, or closed knot.

An Open Mark branches into unfinished tendrils.

Elderwood traditions often describe the distinction as:

the blood took form

for Fixed, and

the blood opened

for Open.

Beastfolk — The Three Claws

A Beastfolk Mark appears as exactly three parallel claw-like lines through fur, feathers, scales, or skin.

A Fixed Mark forms three complete strokes.

An Open Mark contains unfinished, broken, branching, or fading ends.

The Mark is shared across Beastfolk lineages and does not identify temperament, profession, or exact Gift.

Humans — The Hand of Resonance

A human Mark always appears on the back of one hand.

Humans are unusual because their Mark shows both Fixed/Open status and the strongest broad divine resonance associated with the Gift.

Common forms are:

  • Solenne — an eye or radiant point with four rays;
  • Nyvara — a crescent with a star or point;
  • Elyra — a branching tree or mirrored root;
  • Morthain — a gate-like symbol, sometimes with small points or stars.

A Fixed version appears closed and complete.

An Open version remains visibly unfinished, expanding, broken, or unclosed.

The resonance is only a clue. A person might know that a Gift resonates with Morthain without knowing whether the Gift concerns memory, remains, endings, stillness, spirits, preservation, or something else.

Many human traditions interpret this additional information as evidence of special divine favor. Aureonite theology often makes that claim especially strongly. Other cultures dispute it, and there is no universally accepted public explanation.

Dwarves — The Burdened Shoulder

A dwarven Mark appears across a shoulder, shoulder blade, upper arm, or collarbone.

Its pattern resembles flame, embers, rolling shadow, or layered dark bands.

A Fixed Mark settles into a clearly bounded form.

An Open Mark spreads outward through unfinished flame-like or shadow-like extensions.

The pattern does not by itself identify divine resonance.

Demonkin — The Crescent

A Demonkin Mark appears as a crescent on the forehead.

A Fixed Mark is clean and uninterrupted.

An Open Mark contains a break, unfinished inner arc, doubled edge, or fine extensions that keep it from appearing fully closed.

It reveals Fixed versus Open, but not divine resonance.

Fixed Gifts

A Fixed Gift channels supernatural inheritance into one stable, narrow capability.

Examples include:

  • perfect balance;
  • recent-track sensing;
  • exceptional dream memory;
  • finding water;
  • sensing structural faults;
  • calming animals;
  • sensing nearby death.

A Gift does not arrive with an official divine name. People discover what it does and name it themselves.

Fixed Gifts deepen through important story Revelations rather than normal spell learning.

Fixed-Gift characters normally do not possess Mana or ordinary true spellcasting.

Open Gifts

An Open Gift produces Mana and the capacity to learn true magic.

It does not automatically teach spells.

An Open-Gift person still needs:

  • instruction;
  • practice;
  • study;
  • a tradition;
  • experience.

An Open-Gift person may choose never to become a professional mage.

Twice-Touched

Rare stories and records describe people who possess both a Fixed Gift and an Open Gift.

Such people are called Twice-Touched and are associated with extraordinary supernatural circumstances and Old-God blessings.

Twice-Touched status is not a normal starting-character option.

Marks and the Body

Marks may be covered, decorated, tattooed around, scarred, or injured without removing the Gift.

Different cultures have different attitudes toward displaying, hiding, touching, or decorating a Mark.
