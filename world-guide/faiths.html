The supernatural is visibly real in Veyra.

That does not mean theology is simple.

People can witness miracles, spirits, Gifts, sacred places, miasma, relics, and magic while still disagreeing about what those things prove.

A sincere religion can preserve something true while misunderstanding other parts of its own history.

The Four Old Gods

The oldest widely known divine names are:

  • Solenne — Light;
  • Nyvara — Dark;
  • Elyra — Life;
  • Morthain — Death.

Their worship predates the modern kingdoms.

The Four are not arranged into a simple good-versus-evil alignment.

Solenne

Solenne is associated with Light in the broader sense of:

  • truth;
  • revelation;
  • knowledge;
  • precision;
  • direction;
  • order;
  • illumination.

Light can guide and reveal, but revelation can also become harsh, invasive, or obsessive.

Nyvara

Nyvara is associated with Dark:

  • dreams;
  • intuition;
  • secrecy;
  • shadows;
  • hidden knowledge;
  • emotion;
  • protection;
  • stars;
  • concealment.

Dark is not treated as synonymous with evil. Secrecy can protect as easily as it can deceive.

Elyra

Elyra is associated with Life:

  • growth;
  • healing;
  • movement;
  • adaptation;
  • plants;
  • animals;
  • bodily change;
  • becoming.

Life can heal and nourish, but uncontrolled life can also consume, spread, mutate, or overwhelm.

Morthain

Morthain is associated with Death:

  • endings;
  • memory;
  • remains;
  • ancestry;
  • stillness;
  • decay;
  • preservation;
  • passage.

Death is not treated as automatically malicious. An ending can be necessary, merciful, sacred, or part of a larger cycle.

The Old Gods in the Present

Worship of the Four survives through temples, shrines, local traditions, relics, family practice, songs, ancestors, sacred sites, and partial histories.

The Four do not routinely hold clear conversations with priests in the modern age. Different cultures explain that silence in different ways.

Aureon

Aureon is especially powerful in Solvarra.

Public titles include:

  • Father of Man;
  • Perfect Form;
  • First King;
  • Lord of Human Flame;
  • Unbroken Light.

The Church of Aureon teaches order, hierarchy, purpose, civilization, obedience, and divine rule. Some traditions explicitly teach human primacy.

Aureonite practitioners are associated with genuine supernatural effects involving healing, light, courage, wards, strength, resistance to fear, and truth-seeking.

The existence of miracles does not prevent disagreement over what those miracles prove.

Varesh

Varesh is especially influential in Tharakai and some northern Beastfolk traditions.

Titles include:

  • First Beast;
  • Father of Claws;
  • Thousand-Formed;
  • Lord of the Hunt;
  • Blood That Runs Wild.

Varesh is associated with hunting, senses, animals, survival, healing, transformation, instinct, courage, and ferocity.

Different Beastfolk lineages may portray Varesh differently.

Varesh is not the universal god of all Beastfolk.

Ancestors, Spirits, and Local Faiths

Veyra contains countless local religious traditions.

People may honor:

  • ancestors;
  • household spirits;
  • river gods;
  • mountain beings;
  • saints;
  • guardians;
  • city patrons;
  • seasonal powers;
  • local gods;
  • spirits of place.

Remembering an ancestor is not always the same thing as worshipping them.

Not every sacred practice is organized through a centralized church.

Religion in Caelora

Caelora has no national religion.

Citizens may follow the Old Gods, Aureon, Varesh, local deities, ancestors, spirits, foreign religions, or no religion at all.

Public institutions are expected to serve citizens regardless of faith.

Religious Characters

A priest is not automatically a mage.

Open-Gift people may learn magical practices through religious traditions. Fixed-Gift people may become priests, scholars, warriors, healers, or religious leaders without gaining ordinary spellcasting.

A character may also be nonreligious while accepting that supernatural beings and phenomena exist.

The important question is not merely whether something supernatural exists, but what authority—if any—a character believes it deserves.
