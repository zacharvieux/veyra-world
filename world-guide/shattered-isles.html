The Shattered Isles

Broken waters, independent harbors, ancient ruins, and laws that may change before the next tide.

The Shattered Isles lie within the Shattered Sea between Aldara and Caelora. They are the surviving fragments of the region broken by the Sundering: islands, reefs, drowned streets, ruined towers, sealed chambers, broken temples, and structures whose purposes are no longer understood.

A Region Without One Government

No single government rules the Isles. Settlements may be governed by elected councils, merchant groups, harbor authorities, military administrations, local strong figures, religious communities, private interests, or arrangements that make sense only locally.

A traveler is expected to ask who controls a harbor before assuming anything about law, taxes, weapons, salvage, quarantine, or acceptable flags.

The Harbor Board

In many established ports, the first practical source of information is the harbor board: a publicly posted set of current notices identifying the governing authority, harbor fees, quarantine rules, salvage procedures, prohibited flags or vessels, pilotage requirements, emergency signals, and temporary closures.

The board is not a universal institution and may be little more than nailed notices under a roof, but experienced captains check it before they trust yesterday's information.

Who Lives There

Permanent residents include fishers, dockworkers, merchants, shipwrights, researchers, salvagers, innkeepers, guides, hunters, craftspeople, and families who have lived in the islands for generations.

Temporary residents include soldiers, academics, treasure seekers, explorers, smugglers, mercenaries, pilgrims, monster hunters, and adventurers.

Large ports can be among the most culturally mixed places in the known world. Shattered Common absorbs vocabulary freely from sailors and settlers arriving from Aldara, Caelora, Tsukara, and elsewhere.

Daily Life

Fish, shellfish, hard bread, imported grain, stews, preserved meat, and sea plants are common foods. Large ports can offer extremely diverse cuisine because crews arrive from everywhere.

Housing and sanitation vary dramatically. A wealthy trade harbor may have imported pumps, clinics, paved quays, regulated warehouses, and Caeloran equipment, while a small fishing settlement may depend on wells, cisterns, timber buildings, and local healers.

Storms, monsters, supply interruptions, and political changes make emergency stores and practical repair skills valuable almost everywhere.

Salvage and Ruins

Salvagers search for tools, books, artwork, metals, relics, machinery, magical objects, saltstone, and architectural fragments.

Most finds are mundane. Some are not.

Ownership can be disputed among finders, settlements, expedition sponsors, governments, banks, scholars, cultural claimants, and anyone else able to make a case. A legal claim recognized in one harbor may mean nothing in another.

Public theories of the Sundering include magical catastrophe, divine punishment, ancient war, failed ritual magic, and natural disaster amplified by magic. The ruins support certainty that an advanced civilization existed here; they do not provide one universally accepted explanation for its destruction.

Travel and the Sea

The Isles turn sea travel into a sequence of local decisions. Captains must repeatedly ask:

  • Who governs this harbor?
  • What law applies here?
  • Who claims salvage rights?
  • What flags are welcome today?
  • Which waters are unsafe?

Travel through the archipelago may be delayed by weather, pilotage, resupply, inspections, salvage disputes, monsters, or local politics. A captain trusted in one port may be wanted in the next.

Foreign Influence

Caelora is interested in archaeology, research, saltstone, naval security, trade routes, and permanent facilities. Valemere favors finance, contracts, shipping, and salvage markets. Other Aldaran powers sponsor expeditions, mercenaries, religious missions, or political interests.

Tsukara participates in trade, rescue, navigation, exploration, and monster hunting while remaining wary of foreign domination of nearby sea lanes.

Island communities are not passive pieces on a foreign map. Some welcome investment or military protection; others consider the same offer a threat to independence.

Adventure Culture

The Isles attract people who are curious, ambitious, desperate, fleeing something, searching for something, or several of those at once.

That makes them one of the easiest places in Veyra for characters from very different backgrounds to meet—and one of the easiest places to discover that everyone arrived with a different idea of who owns the past.

If someone says, “Nobody has ever opened this door before,” that is not automatically a reason to open it.
