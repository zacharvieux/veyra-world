The Shattered Isles lie within the Shattered Sea between Aldara and Caelora.

They are remnants of the region destroyed by the Sundering: islands, reefs, submerged ruins, broken towers, temples, sealed chambers, drowned streets, and structures whose purposes are no longer understood.

No single government rules the Isles.

Settlements may be governed by:

  • elected councils;
  • merchant groups;
  • harbor authorities;
  • military administrations;
  • local strong figures;
  • other arrangements.

Law can change from harbor to harbor.

Who Lives There

The Shattered Isles are highly mixed.

Permanent residents include fishers, dockworkers, merchants, shipwrights, researchers, salvagers, innkeepers, guides, hunters, and families who have lived there for generations.

Temporary residents include soldiers, academics, treasure seekers, explorers, smugglers, mercenaries, pilgrims, and adventurers.

Salvage and Ruins

Salvagers search for:

  • tools;
  • books;
  • artwork;
  • metals;
  • relics;
  • machinery;
  • magical objects;
  • saltstone;
  • architectural fragments.

Most finds are mundane.

Some are not.

Ownership can be disputed among finders, settlements, expedition sponsors, governments, banks, scholars, cultural claimants, and anyone else able to make a case.

Foreign Influence

Caelora is interested in archaeology, research, saltstone, naval security, trade routes, and permanent facilities.

Valemere favors finance, contracts, shipping, and salvage markets.

Other Aldaran powers sponsor expeditions, mercenaries, religious missions, or political interests.

Tsukara participates in trade, rescue, navigation, exploration, and monster hunting while remaining wary of foreign domination of nearby sea lanes.

Adventure Culture

The Isles attract people who are:

  • curious;
  • ambitious;
  • desperate;
  • fleeing something;
  • searching for something;
  • or several of those at once.

This makes them one of the easiest places in Veyra for characters from completely different backgrounds to meet.

A common piece of island wisdom is:

If someone says, “Nobody has ever opened this door before,” that is not automatically a reason to open it.
