Durakhal

An independent confederation of dwarven mountain Holds where memory, engineering, repair, and written obligation shape public life.

Durakhal sits north of Tharakai and is the best-known independent dwarven homeland in the known world.

Holds and Government

Durakhal is a confederation rather than a tightly centralized kingdom. Individual Holds maintain their own traditions and laws while cooperating on shared defense, trade, roads, tunnels, engineering standards, and disputes that cross Hold boundaries.

The political compact among the Holds was renewed during the post-Sundering Age of New Crowns. Modern Durakhal therefore treats cooperation as something maintained through obligation and record, not assumed by default.

Memory, Craft, and Obligation

Memory. Craft. Obligation.

These ideas carry unusual cultural weight. A promise can create duties that outlive the person who first made it. Family reputation may be built over generations. Repair can be treated as seriously as invention.

Durakhal law gives exceptional weight to written obligation, witness, and recorded precedent. Contract houses are internationally trusted, and falsifying a Hold's assay or weight is not merely fraud; it attacks the Hold's reputation.

Language, Education, and Records

Khordic is the broad dwarven language tradition most strongly associated with Durakhal. Spoken forms vary sharply by Hold, while formal written Khordic changes more slowly because contracts, engineering records, archives, and long institutional continuity reward precision. Old Khordic survives in ancient inscriptions and records.

Recordkeeping is culturally important. Children in established Holds commonly receive basic literacy, arithmetic, safety instruction, and family or Hold history. Professional education often becomes apprenticeship. The result is high literacy in established Holds and a deep expectation that important work should leave records behind.

Engineering and Technology

Mountain life punishes bad construction quickly. Durakhal engineering commonly uses deep ventilation shafts, water drainage, counterweighted lifts, ore carts and track systems, load-bearing arches, emergency bulkheads, firebreaks, structural inspections, detailed maintenance records, and runic locks or wards where trained Open-Gift specialists are available.

The governing design preference is repairability and long service life. An elegant mechanism that cannot be repaired underground may be considered worse than a less efficient one that can.

Durakhal also produces sophisticated prosthetic limbs and mobility devices. Administrative Holds use movable-type printing, and mining communities may possess ventilation, drainage, lifts, and safety systems beyond those of neighboring rural estates.

Roads, Homes, Food, and Work

Durakhal maintains engineered mountain roads, tunnels, switchbacks, bridges, and protected way stations where geography permits. Underground and mountain housing is designed around ventilation, drainage, fire control, structural movement, and evacuation.

Work clothing emphasizes durability, safety, and repair. Mining crews treat first aid and rescue readiness as workplace necessities.

Mountain agriculture cannot support every Hold, so food imports matter. Preserved foods, fungi, hardy vegetables, imported grain, cheese, and meat are common. In return, Durakhal exports metal, tools, engineering expertise, stonework, weapons, armor, and mineral wealth.

Faith and Sacred Practice

Religion is decentralized. Ancestor traditions, household spirits, local gods, mountain practices, Morthain, Nyvara, and other older traditions all have followers. No single Durakhal church defines the entire realm.

Runecraft is especially prominent among dwarven magical traditions, particularly work involving stone, metal, structures, permanence, wards, binding, enchantment, and memory.

Money, Measure, and Time

Durakhal's major trade coins are called seals. Every authorized mint marks the issuing Hold and assay information into the metal, which is one reason foreign merchants trust them.

Dwarven engineering relies on extremely precise standardized references maintained by each Hold and cross-checked through inter-Hold agreements. Some modern Caeloran standards were historically compared against Durakhal reference weights and rods.

Dates commonly use SR alongside Hold-specific archival notation and office terms. A dwarven archivist may be able to date a contract more precisely than the government that originally issued it.
