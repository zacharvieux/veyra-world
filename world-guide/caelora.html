Caelora is a continent-sized nation containing many climates, peoples, industries, and regional identities.

A person from the frozen Northern Reach and someone raised in the waterways of the Southern Fenlands may live very different lives while both strongly identify as Caeloran.

National identity is reinforced through:

  • the Crown;
  • public institutions;
  • schools;
  • law;
  • the Common Standard;
  • standardized administration;
  • roads and ports;
  • the Signal Line;
  • Arcrail.

The Steward Monarchy

Caelora is ruled by a monarchy built around stewardship rather than unrestricted royal privilege.

The governing principle is:

The country does not exist to enrich the Crown.
The Crown exists to preserve the country.

The Crown Steward is expected to protect public welfare, food security, medicine, education, commerce, transportation, infrastructure, peace, and national defense.

The Crown is checked by law, public expectations, regional representation, and professional oversight.

The Common Standard

Every Caeloran citizen is guaranteed access in principle to:

  • basic safe housing;
  • sufficient food;
  • clean water;
  • basic medicine;
  • education;
  • sanitation;
  • basic utilities;
  • public transportation;
  • legal protections.

The Common Standard does not eliminate wealth, class, political influence, luxury, or regional inequality. It does mean that the government is expected to treat failure of basic public systems as a public responsibility rather than private misfortune.

The system works best near major population centers and developed Arcrail routes. Remote and frontier regions can experience weaker services, delays, local corruption, and stronger regional power.

Caeloran Confidence

Caeloran culture often assumes that problems can be solved:

Build a road.
Fund a school.
Research the disease.
Engineer the bridge.
Change the law.
Develop the technology.

That confidence produces extraordinary achievements.

It can also become arrogance, especially when Caelorans assume that systems which improved life at home give them the right to reshape neighboring societies.
