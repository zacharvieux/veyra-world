Aldara

The western continent is an old world of kingdoms, Holds, clan lands, trade roads, rivers, faiths, and inherited rivalries.

Aldara is politically fragmented, culturally diverse, and deeply shaped by distance.

No Aldaran realm is completely self-sufficient. Trade is one of the major reasons old rivals continue speaking to one another, even when religion, politics, memory, and territorial claims pull them apart.

Distance, Identity, and Borders

Aldara is the great western continent: old kingdoms, clan lands, mountain Holds, river valleys, forests, ports, temples, trade roads, and inherited rivalries. Distance still shapes politics. Crossing a kingdom can take weeks by horse, and crossing much of the continent can take months.

People often identify as strongly with a town, clan, Hold, guild, profession, faith, or family as they do with a kingdom. Political borders are not always perfectly agreed lines. Different maps may disagree about grazing rights, forests, river access, fortifications, and ancestral holdings.

The modern political order took recognizable form during the Age of New Crowns after the Sundering. Solvarra's crown-and-church institutions emerged, Ardaren's noble houses consolidated river and cavalry territories, Valemere's trade cities coordinated commercial law, Karthain and Tharakai developed stronger shared institutions, and Durakhal renewed the political compact among its Holds.

Languages and Written Culture

Common is widely used across Aldaran trade and diplomacy, but its dialects carry strong regional identities. Solvarran Common is formal and shaped by hierarchy and religion. Ardaren Common varies across rural, noble, river, and merchant communities. Valemerian Common is highly international and full of borrowed vocabulary.

Aldara also contains major non-Common language traditions. Khordic is strongly associated with Durakhal; Tharic with Tharakai; Karthic with Karthain; and Aelthiric with the Elderwood. None of these languages belongs automatically to every member of an ancestry.

Multilingualism is especially common among merchants, sailors, diplomats, scholars, border communities, and residents of major ports.

Roads, Rivers, and Travel

Aldara remains largely pre-industrial in its travel. Road quality differs sharply by realm. Solvarra maintains disciplined military and tax roads; Ardaren invests heavily in roads linking rivers, estates, markets, and cavalry centers; Valemere's best roads are freight-and-toll arteries; Karthain combines permanent roads with seasonal tracks, clan paths, and hunting corridors; Tharakai relies heavily on local route knowledge; and Durakhal builds engineered mountain roads, tunnels, switchbacks, bridges, and protected way stations.

Where population and security allow, major roads support wayhouses roughly a day's travel apart. They may provide food, water, stables, beds, courier exchange, guards, news, and information about hazards. An abandoned wayhouse is often a warning that a road has become unsafe.

Rivers are among Aldara's most important freight systems. Grain, timber, stone, metal, wine, livestock products, and other bulk goods move more cheaply by barge than by road, giving river cities influence far beyond their population.

Daily Life Across Aldara

There is no single Aldaran lifestyle. Rural homes commonly use local timber, stone, brick, plaster, thatch, or tile. Water comes from wells, springs, cisterns, and local systems. Dense cities may contain multi-story housing, workshops, guild quarters, temples, public wells, bathhouses, courtyards, and markets.

Food follows geography. Temperate regions commonly rely on bread, grain porridge, beans, roots, cheese, preserved meat, river fish, fruit, ale, cider, and wine where climate permits. Solvarra and Ardaren are major agricultural regions. Northern diets lean more heavily on livestock, preserved foods, hardy grain, roots, smoked meat, stews, hunting, and gathering. Valemere's trade centers offer imported spices, seafood, preserved goods, and foreign dishes.

Sanitation varies sharply. Large cities may have drains, public latrines, wells, cesspits, and waste collection, but wealthier districts are often cleaner than poorer or rapidly growing ones. Skilled surgery and medicine can require days of travel from the countryside.

Technology, Craft, and Information

Most of Aldara operates at an advanced pre-industrial level: waterwheels, windmills, steel tools and weapons, cranes, pulleys, river barges, improved agriculture, glassmaking, clocks in wealthier towns, siege engines, printing, fortified roads, and regional magical crafts.

The continent is not technologically uniform. A Valemerian banking district may use imported Caeloran communications while a nearby village still waits for a weekly rider. Durakhal engineering can exceed neighboring states in mining and structural systems. Elderwood craft can appear simple while depending on magical and ecological techniques outsiders cannot reproduce.

Movable-type printing is common in Valemere, Solvarra, major Ardaren cities, and Durakhal administrative Holds. What gets printed differs: contracts and newspapers in Valemere, doctrine and approved histories in Solvarra, and administrative or archival material in Durakhal.

Personal firearms remain extraordinarily rare. Imported or experimental gunpowder weapons exist, but reliable powder, barrels, mechanisms, and standardized ammunition are difficult and expensive to sustain.

Law, Faith, and Local Authority

Aldara has no single legal order. A traveler is responsible for knowing where they are. Solvarra divides authority between Crown and Church. Valemere emphasizes property, guild law, contracts, and commercial courts. Durakhal gives unusual weight to written obligation, witness, and recorded precedent. Clan custom and local authority remain important across the north.

Faith is similarly diverse. The Four Old Gods remain present through shrines, local traditions, relics, sacred sites, family practice, and partial histories. Aureon is especially influential in Solvarra and widely worshipped in Ardaren. Varesh is especially influential in Tharakai and some northern Beastfolk traditions. Ancestors, household spirits, river gods, mountain beings, saints, guardians, and local deities are common across the continent.

Money, Measures, and Time

Aldaran states mint their own currencies and maintain their own customs. International merchants often compare value using Trade Silver, but actual payment may use local coin, foreign currency, bank credit, or bullion.

Solvarra uses coppers, silver rays, and high-value crowns. Ardaren's silver riders grew out of cavalry pay. Valemere relies heavily on coin, bank drafts, letters of credit, warehouse receipts, and its silver-denominated vale. Durakhal's trusted seals carry Hold and assay marks. Karthain and Tharakai mix local silver, bullion, foreign coin, livestock valuation, and frontier exchange.

Sundering Reckoning is increasingly common in international business, but local dating remains alive. Solvarra also uses an Aureonite liturgical calendar and regnal years; Ardaren may date rural events by reigns, harvests, wars, or floods; Valemere strongly favors SR for contracts; Durakhal adds Hold archival notation; Karthain and Tharakai retain seasonal and clan dating; and Elderwood communities may use seasonal, astronomical, biological, and event-based references.
