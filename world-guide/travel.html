Travel & the Known World

Veyra is enormous. Distance should matter until technology changes what distance means.

The known world is large enough that most people never see all of it.

Aldara and Caelora are continental landmasses. Tsukara is a major southern island civilization. Between Aldara and Caelora lies the Shattered Sea and its politically fragmented islands. To the far east and west, the Uncrossed Mists define the known world’s practical limits.

The Scale of Aldara

Aldara is an old-road continent.

Travel depends on:

  • feet;
  • horses;
  • wagons;
  • rivers;
  • coastal ships;
  • maintained roads; and
  • local hospitality.

Crossing even one kingdom can require weeks.

Crossing much of Aldara can require months.

That matters politically.

A king may issue an order that takes days or weeks to reach a distant border.

A village may hear about a war after the first battle is already over.

A merchant’s knowledge of road conditions can be worth as much as their cargo.

Ordinary Travel Rates

These are intended as world-guide defaults rather than rigid tactical rules:

MethodTypical sustained travel
Walking on a good road15–22 miles/day
Walking through difficult country8–15 miles/day
Wagon / freight caravan12–20 miles/day
Riding horse, sustainable journey25–35 miles/day
Fast mounted courier with relays60–90 miles/day
River travel downstreamhighly variable, often faster than road freight

Weather, terrain, border delays, monsters, road quality, and injury matter more than perfect arithmetic.

Aldaran Roads

Road quality varies by kingdom.

Solvarra maintains unusually disciplined military and tax roads.

Ardaren invests heavily in roads connecting river valleys, estates, markets, and cavalry centers.

Valemere’s best roads are commercial arteries designed around freight and toll traffic.

Karthain’s routes include permanent roads, seasonal tracks, clan paths, and hunting corridors.

Tharakai has fewer uniformly maintained highways but extensive local knowledge of routes through difficult northern land.

Durakhal maintains engineered mountain roads, tunnels, switchbacks, bridges, and protected way stations where geography permits.

Wayhouses

Across much of Aldara, major roads support wayhouses approximately a day’s travel apart where population and security allow.

A wayhouse may provide:

  • food;
  • water;
  • stable space;
  • beds or common sleeping rooms;
  • courier exchange;
  • local news;
  • guards; and
  • information about road hazards.

They range from royal stations to private inns to fortified compounds.

A ruined or abandoned wayhouse is a common sign that a road has become unsafe.

River Travel

Rivers are among Aldara’s most important transport systems.

Heavy freight moves more cheaply by water than by road.

Barges carry:

  • grain;
  • timber;
  • stone;
  • metal;
  • wine;
  • livestock products; and
  • bulk trade goods.

River cities therefore possess economic influence far beyond their populations.

The Elderwood

Travel rules change inside Aelthirien.

There is no accurate conventional map of the interior.

Paths shift. Streams alter. Distances may not behave consistently.

Safe deep travel generally requires either:

  • an elf who genuinely knows the forest; or
  • a blessing recognized by the Old Gods.

This is not simply a navigation check against an unusually complicated forest.

The Elderwood behaves differently from ordinary geography.

Caelora by Rail

Caelora is too large to function politically the way it does without Arcrail.

The developed network makes journeys of continental scale possible in days.

Central trunk lines are frequent, maintained, and heavily regulated.

Secondary routes may require transfers.

Frontier service can be slow or unreliable.

Freight and passenger priority can change during:

  • war;
  • miasma events;
  • crop failures;
  • evacuations;
  • major storms; or
  • royal emergencies.

Travel Examples

Instead of giving every city an exact distance before the canonical map is finalized, the guide can use examples:

  • A journey that takes three weeks by mounted travel in Aldara might take a day or less on a major Caeloran Arcrail corridor of comparable distance.
  • A trip across a large portion of Caelora may require two to five days with transfers.
  • Reaching an isolated frontier community may still require days or weeks after leaving the railhead.

This preserves the scale without locking an unfinished map into false precision.

Tickets and Rail Travel

Passenger Arcrail service is divided more by comfort and scheduling than by whether ordinary people are allowed to travel.

The Common Standard supports basic mobility inside Caelora, but it does not mean every citizen receives unlimited luxury travel.

Common ticket categories include:

  • public/basic seating;
  • reserved long-distance seating;
  • sleeper compartments; and
  • private or official compartments.

Freight, medical evacuation, military traffic, and government relief can take priority.

Skybarque Travel

Skybarques are valuable because they do not need continuous ground infrastructure.

They are common for:

  • wealthy passengers;
  • survey teams;
  • officials;
  • emergency medicine;
  • remote engineering teams;
  • military scouting; and
  • expeditions.

They are not the normal way ordinary people move bulk goods across Caelora.

Sea Travel

The Shattered Sea is both a barrier and a highway.

Major routes connect:

  • Valemere;
  • Caelora’s Dawning Coast;
  • the Shattered Isles;
  • Tsukara; and
  • smaller Aldaran ports.

Conventional sailing vessels remain common.

Tsukaran ships are respected for navigation and storm handling.

Caeloran Stonewakes dominate some heavy commercial and naval routes.

Voyage Scale

Without locking exact port locations, the following voyage ranges are established for campaign scale:

  • Major Aldara-to-Caelora Stonewake routes: typically 5–10 days in good conditions.
  • Conventional sailing routes across comparable distances: often 2–3 weeks, sometimes longer.
  • Travel through the Shattered Isles can add substantial delay because ships may stop for weather, pilotage, resupply, salvage law, or local politics.

These are campaign-facing approximations, not promises that every coast-to-coast route has identical distance.

Tsukaran Travel

Tsukaran citizens travel widely outside their homeland.

Foreign travel inside Tsukara is more restricted.

Foreign merchants are welcome in approved ports, while access to:

  • sacred islands;
  • military facilities;
  • navigation stations;
  • protected communities; and
  • dangerous regions

may require permission or be prohibited entirely.

Travel Seals

Foreigners permitted beyond open port districts receive a travel seal identifying:

  • the holder;
  • permitted region or purpose;
  • issuing authority; and
  • expiration.

The seal may be a stamped paper, durable tag, or formal document depending on purpose.

Losing one is inconvenient.

Forging one is a serious crime.

The Shattered Isles

The Isles are not one country.

Travelers must repeatedly ask:

  • Who governs this harbor?
  • What law applies here?
  • Who claims salvage rights?
  • What flags are currently welcome?
  • Which waters are unsafe?

Some ports are orderly commercial centers.

Others are military posts, research stations, fishing settlements, private holdings, or criminal havens.

A captain trusted in one port may be wanted in the next.

Passports and Borders

Veyra does not possess one universal passport system.

Travel documents depend on destination.

Common examples include:

  • letters of passage;
  • merchant guild identification;
  • military orders;
  • diplomatic papers;
  • ship manifests;
  • Caeloran civic identification;
  • Tsukaran travel seals; and
  • temple or scholarly letters of introduction.

Valemere recognizes a particularly broad range of foreign papers because trade benefits from making entry predictable.

Solvarra applies greater scrutiny to foreigners and nonhuman travelers.

Rural borders elsewhere may be little more than a bridge toll and a bored guard.

Communication

Travel and communication are not the same thing.

In Aldara, important messages commonly move through:

  • mounted couriers;
  • merchant networks;
  • river traffic;
  • temple messengers;
  • military posts;
  • printed notices; and
  • ships.

Caelora’s Signal Line allows important written messages to outrun physical travel dramatically.

Tsukara uses excellent naval dispatch systems, signal flags, lights, coded harbor signals, and precise scheduling.

International Postal Contracts

Valemerian merchant houses and Caeloran postal authorities maintain reciprocal contracts allowing sealed letters to transfer between ship, rail, and courier networks without being renegotiated at every border.

This is expensive compared with local mail but extremely useful to merchants, scholars, and adventurers.

A letter from central Caelora can sometimes reach Valemere before a traveler who departed on the same day.

The Uncrossed Mists

The Mists are not merely bad weather.

Near them:

  • saltstone devices fail;
  • engines misfire;
  • crystals dim or overload;
  • wards crack;
  • compasses spin;
  • clocks drift;
  • maps become unreliable.

Deeper inside, even ordinary magic and perceived distance can behave inconsistently.

Straightforward mechanical tools, sail, rope, muscle, and fire may remain dependable longer than sophisticated magical systems.

No publicly verified expedition is known to have crossed the Mists and returned.

Tsukaran tradition claims an ancestral Crossing Through the White, but this is religious and historical tradition rather than independently verified modern navigation.
